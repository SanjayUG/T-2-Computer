## 3. Generations of Computers

| Generation | Period | Main Technology |
|---|---|---|
| First | 1940–1956 | Vacuum Tubes |
| Second | 1956–1963 | Transistors |
| Third | 1964–1971 | Integrated Circuits (ICs) |
| Fourth | 1971–Present | Microprocessors (VLSI) |
| Fifth | Present & Beyond | ULSI, AI and Quantum Computing |

### First Generation (1940–1956)

- Technology: Vacuum Tubes and Magnetic Drums.
- Very large, expensive and slow.
- Consumed high power and produced heat.
- Used Machine Language.

**Examples:** ENIAC, EDVAC, UNIVAC-I, IBM-701.

### Second Generation (1956–1963)

- Technology: Transistors.
- Smaller, faster and more reliable.
- Less power consumption and heat.
- Used Assembly Language, COBOL and FORTRAN.

**Examples:** IBM 1401, IBM 7094, CDC 1604.

### Third Generation (1964–1971)

- Technology: Integrated Circuits (ICs).
- Smaller, faster and cheaper.
- Supported Operating Systems and multiprogramming.
- Used languages like BASIC, C and Pascal.

**Examples:** IBM 360, PDP-8, Honeywell 6000.

### Fourth Generation (1971–Present)

- Technology: Microprocessors (VLSI).
- Smaller, portable and affordable.
- Development of GUI, networking and Internet.
- Used C, C++, Java and .NET.

**Examples:** IBM PC, Apple Macintosh, Intel 4004, Pentium.

### Fifth Generation (Present & Beyond)

- Technology: ULSI, AI chips and Quantum Computing.
- Focus on Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning.
- Uses Robotics, NLP and Expert Systems.
- Supports parallel processing and self-learning.

**Examples:** IBM Watson, Google DeepMind, Quantum Computers.
