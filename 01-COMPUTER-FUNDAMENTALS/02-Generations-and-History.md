## 3. Five Generations of Computers

| Generation | Period | Technology |
|---|---|---|
| First | 1940–1956 | Vacuum Tubes |
| Second | 1956–1963 | Transistors |
| Third | 1964–1971 | Integrated Circuits (ICs) |
| Fourth | 1971–Present | Microprocessors (VLSI) |
| Fifth | Present & Beyond | ULSI, AI |

### First Generation
- Technology: Vacuum Tubes, Magnetic Drums.
- Features: Large, expensive, slow, high power consumption.
- Language: Machine Language.
- Examples: ENIAC, EDVAC, UNIVAC-I, IBM-701.

### Second Generation
- Technology: Transistors.
- Features: Smaller, faster, reliable, less heat.
- Languages: Assembly, COBOL, FORTRAN.
- Examples: IBM 1401, IBM 7094, CDC 1604.

### Third Generation
- Technology: Integrated Circuits (ICs).
- Features: Smaller, faster, cheaper; multiprogramming.
- Languages: BASIC, C, Pascal.
- Examples: IBM 360, PDP-8, Honeywell 6000.

### Fourth Generation
- Technology: Microprocessors (VLSI).
- Features: Personal computers, GUI, networking.
- Languages: C, C++, Java.
- Examples: IBM PC, Apple Macintosh, Intel 4004, Pentium.

### Fifth Generation
- Technology: ULSI, AI chips, Quantum Computing.
- Features: AI, Machine Learning, Robotics, NLP.
- Examples: IBM Watson, Google DeepMind, Quantum Computers.
